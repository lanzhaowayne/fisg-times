Trust & Safety

Regulation and Security

When selecting a regulated forex broker, security is non-negotiable. The licensing authority, fund segregation model, and negative balance protection all determine how well traders are protected when market conditions become extreme.

What to Verify When Choosing a Regulated Broker
Licensing authorities — Look for regulated brokers holding licences from CySEC, ASIC, or equivalent tier-1 regulators that enforce strict compliance standards.
Segregation of client funds — Client deposits should be held in separate accounts, independent from the broker's operating capital.
Negative balance protection — Ensures your losses can never exceed your deposited funds, even in highly volatile market conditions.
Security remains one of the key factors separating reliable brokers from risky ones — always verify regulation before depositing funds.

Costs

Costs and Trading Conditions

Understanding pricing is critical to long-term profitability. Even small differences in spread or commission accumulate significantly over hundreds of trades. Many traders compare FISG spread conditions when evaluating cost efficiency across brokers.

Key Cost Factors to Evaluate
Competitive spreads — The tighter the spread, the lower your cost per trade. Compare raw spreads across asset classes before committing to a platform.
Low commissions — Some brokers offer commission-free trading on wider spreads; others charge a fixed commission with near-zero spreads — know which model suits your strategy.
Transparent overnight fees — Swap rates on leveraged positions held overnight should be clearly disclosed and competitive with market benchmarks.
